# Auckland organic-traffic topic recommendation

**Research date:** 4 August 2026
**Decision:** Launch the first search-led content pillar around **pool cost and
budget in Auckland**. Use the address-based **Property Check** as its primary
next action. Build property suitability and consents as supporting pages, not
as the first traffic bet.

> **Decision update — 4 August 2026:** The product does not yet have reliable,
> authorised Auckland price ranges. The current launch is therefore
> **Property-Check-First**: lead with the existing Auckland Property Check and
> useful local planning information. Revisit the cost pillar once defensible
> pricing evidence exists; do not publish a price guide in the meantime.

## What this conclusion means

| Topic | Likely discovery reach | Fit with the product | Launch role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pool cost / budget | Broadest early-research question | Good: budget is a natural first step before a site check | **Primary traffic pillar** |
| Can my property suit a pool? | Narrower, but higher-intent | Excellent: this is the useful thing the product can do | **Primary conversion page and CTA** |
| Consents / rules | Important, specific and long-tail | Useful, but time-sensitive and not a substitute for Council advice | **Supporting trust and FAQ cluster** |

Exact Auckland monthly search volumes, click-through rates, and ranking
difficulty are **not verified in this note**. They are not publicly available
from the official sources reviewed. Google provides Keyword Planner for
keyword research and Search Console for a site's actual query impressions and
clicks; those should be used with an authorised account to validate this
qualitative priority before making a large content investment.

## Why cost should lead

A person asking what a pool costs is usually at the earliest viable decision
point: they are deciding whether a project is realistic at all. That makes an
honest Auckland budget guide a broad, non-technical doorway into the subject.
It can answer cost drivers without pretending every property or quote will be
the same, then offer the next useful question: *what may affect this property's
pool plan?*

Suggested launch page and path:

1. **`How much does a pool cost in Auckland?`** - explain the cost components,
   the choices that change them, and the things that cannot be estimated
   without a site/property check. Do not publish a single "from" price without
   a date, scope and source.
2. **CTA: `Check my Auckland property`** - address-first entry to the existing
   preliminary Property Check.
3. **`Can my Auckland property suit a pool?`** - explain the early checks for
   space, access, slope, mapped hazards, services and planning context. Make
   the tool's preliminary/uncertain evidence visible; it is not a survey,
   design or approval.
4. **`Do I need consent for a pool in Auckland?`** - a maintained explainer
   that sends people to current MBIE and Auckland Council guidance.

This deliberately separates **traffic capture** (cost) from **the reason to
choose this site rather than read a generic guide** (a tailored property
check). It also keeps the homepage's main promise practical: "Get an early
view of your property before you plan a pool."

## Evidence and content boundaries

- MBIE says residential pool building work generally needs a building consent
  unless it meets a Schedule 1 exemption; a barrier still needs consent even
  where the pool work is exempt. This makes consent content genuinely useful,
  but it is a high-accuracy, maintained topic rather than the most distinctive
  acquisition page. [MBIE: Building work involving residential pools](https://www.building.govt.nz/building-code-compliance/f-safety-of-users/pool-safety/residential-pool-provisions-of-the-building-act-2004-guidance-for-territorial-authorities/building-work-involving-residential-pools)
- MBIE's homeowner guidance says pools that can hold 400 mm or more need a
  physical barrier, including temporary and inflatable pools, and tells owners
  to check with their local council. [MBIE: Safety guidance for pool owners](https://www.building.govt.nz/building-code-compliance/f-safety-of-users/pool-safety/guidance-for-pool-owners)
- Auckland Council explicitly routes pool and spa owners to consent, inspection
  and safety information. Its pool lodgement checklist also identifies
  property-specific complications such as public drains, flood/soil hazards,
  vegetation and resource-consent conditions. That supports a cautious,
Auckland-specific Property Check rather than a blanket "you can build"
  claim. [Auckland Council: building and renovation projects](https://www.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/en/building-and-consents/building-renovation-projects.html), [Auckland Council: swimming/spa pool lodgement checklist (PDF)](https://www.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/building-and-consents/Documents/ac1032-lodgement-checklist-swimming-spa-pool.pdf)
- Council publishes flood information through Flood Viewer and GeoMaps, but
  describes it as public hazard information rather than a site decision.
  Property results must retain the product's existing preliminary and
  `Needs Checking` language. [Auckland Council: regional flood maps](https://ourauckland.aucklandcouncil.govt.nz/news/2026/03/understanding-auckland-s-regional-flood-maps/)
- LINZ provides reusable aerial imagery, property and boundary data; this
  makes the address-led experience a credible product seam, not a reason to
  overstate its certainty. [LINZ Data Service](https://www.linz.govt.nz/products-services/data/linz-data-service)

## How to verify after launch

1. Create a small, transparent budget guide plus the two supporting pages;
   connect every page to the Property Check with clear consent/privacy copy.
2. In Google Keyword Planner, compare Auckland-targeted variants of `pool cost
   auckland`, `swimming pool cost nz`, `can I build a pool on my property`, and
   `pool consent auckland`. Save the date, targeting and the returned ranges;
   do not present those estimates as actual traffic.
3. Add Google Search Console at launch. Review weekly impressions, clicks,
   average position and Property Check starts by landing page/query. Google
   documents these as the site-specific source of truth for Google Search
   performance. [Google Ads: Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243), [Google Search Console: Performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553)
4. After 8-12 weeks of sufficient impressions, promote the topic with the best
combination of qualified search traffic and Property Check starts - not raw
   visits alone.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not call a Property Check a feasibility approval, consent determination,
  survey, engineer's assessment, or builder quote.
- Keep all price material dated, scoped and sourced; costs vary materially by
  site, pool type, access, earthworks, fencing, consent and services.
- Review consent/rule pages whenever MBIE or Auckland Council changes its
  guidance. Link to the authoritative pages rather than recreating every rule.
